A little about me

Learning, experimenting, and improving.

I’m at the beginning of my journey in computing, and I enjoy the process of figuring out how things work. A new tool or programming idea can feel unfamiliar at first, so I try to make progress by breaking a challenge into smaller pieces, testing one idea at a time, and learning from what happens.

My current work is focused on the foundations: understanding how computers represent information, practicing logical problem solving, and learning how websites are structured. In CS8, I’m getting hands-on experience with HTML and CSS. I’m learning that a strong website is more than a collection of colors and text: it needs a clear structure, readable content, useful navigation, and a layout that works on different screens.

I like projects that give me a chance to create something visible from an idea. Building a page means making many small decisions: what a visitor should notice first, how related information should be grouped, which labels are easiest to understand, and how to keep the design consistent. Each choice is an opportunity to think about the person who will use the result.

I’m also interested in the way creativity and technical thinking work together. A visual design can make information easier to explore, while careful structure helps the design remain accessible and maintainable. I’m practicing both sides by trying ideas, revising details, and paying attention to how the final page feels as a whole.

This portfolio is a snapshot of that learning process. The projects here are early examples, and I plan to keep improving them as I gain experience. I want to continue exploring web development and other areas of technology, take on new challenges, and build a stronger foundation with each project. For now, I’m focused on staying curious, asking good questions, and turning what I learn into something I can share.
